Hiragana & Katakana

Japanese uses two syllabaries alongside kanji. Hiragana writes native words, grammar, and verb endings; katakana writes loanwords, onomatopoeia, and emphasis. They represent exactly the same sounds — learning one teaches you the readings for the other.

Basic hiragana

The forty-six basic characters, read left to right in rows of five. Every other kana in the language is built from these.

あa いi うu えe おo
かka きki くku けke こko

Dakuten and handakuten

Two small marks change how a character is voiced. Dakuten, a pair of strokes in the upper right, turns k into g, s into z, t into d, and h into b. Handakuten, a small circle, turns h into p.

Yōon

A full-size character followed by a small ゃ, ゅ, or ょ fuses into a single syllable. Note that the small character is written at reduced size — きや is two syllables, きゃ is one.
