The story behind Gogaku.

Why Gogaku exists

Most Japanese learning apps are built to keep you opening the app. Gogaku is built to help you actually learn Japanese — and then get out of your way.

The difference matters. Apps optimized for engagement push streaks, notifications, and dopamine loops that make you feel like you're learning. Apps optimized for learning give you the tools, get out of the way, and let you do the work. Gogaku is the second kind.

No streaks. No leaderboards. No owls guilt-tripping you at 9pm. Just structured lessons, a real dictionary, smart flashcards, and a conversation partner — built to work together.

What makes it different

One app, four tools

Serious learners juggle three or four apps: a textbook app for grammar, a dictionary for lookups, a flashcard app for vocabulary, and something for conversation practice. None of them talk to each other. Gogaku puts all four under one roof, and — more importantly — they share a brain. Words you learn in a lesson appear in your flashcard deck. Your conversation partner knows which grammar you've covered. Your dictionary remembers what you've looked up.

A conversation partner that stays in bounds

This is the hard part, and it's the reason Gogaku exists at all. Most language chatbots answer a beginner in fluent, natural Japanese — which is useless if you can't read it. Tomodachi is constrained to the vocabulary and grammar you have actually studied. It builds its sentences from your word list, not from everything it knows. When it does reach for something new, it says so, glosses it, and offers it to your deck.

Built progressively, not randomly

Every lesson stands on the last. Nothing throws grammar at you that you haven't seen. Your flashcards focus on what you've actually encountered. It's the difference between a curriculum and a feed.

Honest pricing

Gogaku is a subscription. Before you pay anything, you get the first lesson in full, the complete dictionary, the kana reference, and up to five flashcard decks you build yourself — enough to judge whether the app is any good, not a demo that stops mid-sentence.

Beyond that it's $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year, starting with a seven-day free trial. Cancel any time from your Apple ID settings; the app will never make you email someone to get out of it. There's no lifetime tier, and that's deliberate — conversation practice costs real money to run every time you use it, and a one-time payment for an ongoing cost is a promise that eventually gets broken.

Who built it

Gogaku is made by one person, working under Philomath Tech LLC in Austin, Texas. No investors, no growth team, no roadmap dictated by engagement metrics. The upside is that every decision in the app is a considered one. The downside is that it ships at the pace one person can build it carefully.
